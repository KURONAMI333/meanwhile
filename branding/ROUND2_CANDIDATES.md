# Meanwhile store identity — round 2 candidates

Round 1's four marks were rejected in full, B most of all. This round is not a
retouch of them: they are kept in `icon-candidates/` as the record of that
decision, and nothing here reuses their thinking.

**Open this to choose:** `branding/icon-candidates-r2/_contact_sheet.png`
(each candidate at 340px with 96 / 64 / 48px insets, its claim, and its
measurements). Then `branding/icon-candidates-r2/_small_size_check.png` for the
same four at 64 and 48px on a light list row and a dark one.

Everything is regenerated by `python render_meanwhile_icons_r2.py`.

## What changed in the thinking

Round 1 reasoned: Subject-Max wants pixel art of the mod's representative block
or item; this mod registers neither; therefore there is no subject; therefore the
mark is abstract. The last step does not follow. Two finished icons in this
family draw a scene built on a subject their mod never registers —
`mod-066-map-art-maker` draws an easel, `mod-030-sky-world` draws floating
islands, and there is no "sky world block". A third, `mod-062-welcome-board`,
burns the function into the field as type after nine abstract candidates were
turned down.

Meanwhile has a subject without registering anything: **a base that kept working
while nobody was there.**

**Where this conflicts with LOGO_PLAYBOOK, said out loud rather than quietly
resolved.** The playbook's gate ⓪ (measure the shelf first) was satisfied in
round 1: 26 icons, three cohorts, shelf bbox median 77%. What that measurement
found on the shelf was one bold silhouette on a plain field, baked text, and
pixel-art objects — **no scenes**. So the shelf does not support a scene; the
family does. kura rejecting four shelf-register marks is what breaks the tie, and
the measurement is not re-run, because a second pass would return the same three
registers.

## The four

They are separated by **register**, not by moment in the story. Two night
exteriors with smoke would be one candidate in two colourways, not two.

| | register | claim about the mod | bbox | coverage | flat colours |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A_room** | isometric diorama (new to this family) | one chunk of your base, still working with nobody standing in it | 100% | 48% | 25 |
| **B_distance** | landscape with depth (`sky-world`) | the part of the world you walked out of keeps running | 100% | 78% | 19 |
| **C_house** | one drawn object on a flat field (`map-art-maker`) | the lights are on, nobody is home, and the work goes on | 99% | 57% | 24 |
| **D_letters** | type burned into the field (`welcome-board`) | *meanwhile* — elsewhere, at the same time, which is the mod | 99% | 17% | 8 |

Family measured the same way: sky-world 8 flat colours, map-art-maker 23,
welcome-board 3. welcome-board's approved icon covers 17–22% of its field, which
is where D sits.

None is a clock, an hourglass or a stopwatch. The one mod on the shelf doing this
job already owns the hourglass.

### Strongest / weakest

- **A_room** — strongest: it is the only one that shows machines actually
  mid-job, and the only candidate with real depth; the empty floor is the
  absence. Weakest: it is the least legible at 48px, where it reduces to a
  grey-and-brown block with one warm speck, and an isometric build is the
  furthest thing from what the shelf around it looks like.
- **B_distance** — strongest: it carries the most mod-specific claim (the world
  you are *not* in is what keeps running), and the smoke column survives the
  reduction better than any other single element here. Weakest: the base itself
  is a speck at 48px, so what survives is "a dusk landscape", which many mods
  could own; a chunk-loader mod could claim this drawing too.
- **C_house** — strongest: by far the clearest at 48px — house, two lit windows,
  light on the ground, all still readable. Weakest: "lit windows at night" is the
  most generic image of the four, and nothing in it says *machines*; it could
  read as a homes/decoration mod.
- **D_letters** — strongest: it says the name, it is the only one that scales
  into a wordmark and a banner without redrawing, and it follows the register
  that actually got approved for welcome-board. Weakest: it claims nothing on its
  own — a reader who does not already know the word learns nothing — and it is
  the tightest fit in the frame (see below).

### The 48px finding for D

Nine glyphs cannot both fit the square and stay legible at 48px. Measured, not
guessed: at glyph scale 3 a stroke is 1.1px once the icon is 48px and the word
turns to grey mush; at scale 5 `WHILE` overruns the frame. Scale 4 is the only
value that works, and at scale 4 with a uniform five-column face the word leaves
5% margin, which reads as cramped. Two fixes are in: `M` and `W` keep five
columns for their diagonals and every other cap is condensed to four (margin back
to ~12%), and the comic caption rule the direction started with is **dropped from
the icon** — at the size the letters need, any inset rule is cut by the corner
radius. The caption rule survives on the banner, where there is room for it.

## How far the identity goes past the icon

kura asked for more branding, so each direction ships three things:

- `icon-candidates-r2/meanwhile_<name>_{512,256,128,64,48}.png`
- `wordmark-candidates/meanwhile_<name>_wordmark.png` — 1280×320, icon beside the
  name on that direction's own field
- `banner-candidates/meanwhile_<name>_banner_1456x728.png` — the 2:1 featured
  gallery image, which Modrinth renders 456×160 in list rows and 260×130 on the
  project page

The banner is not an invented format: it is the second author-controlled
discovery asset the store gives us (STORE_DESCRIPTION_GUIDE), and it matters more
for this mod than for its siblings. Meanwhile is server-side with no screen, no
block and no item, so **there is no in-game screenshot that shows it working** —
a drawn banner is the only honest gallery image it can have.

Every direction extended to a banner. D gains the most from the wider frame (the
name fits on one line and the caption rule comes back); A gains the least, since
the diorama is square by nature and leaves the right half of the banner carrying
only the wordmark.

## Quality gates

`lint` (structure: orphan pixels, nubs, dents, suspect colours) — **findings=0 on
all four.**

`critique --kind item` — **NG on all four** (grade 51 / 51 / 59 / 59), and the
readings are reported rather than acted on, because the same gate run on this
family's three shipped, kura-approved icons returns the same failure with the
same codes: sky-world grade 43, map-art-maker 51, welcome-board 59. The gate is
calibrated on 16×16 in-game textures (N=8,828), and on a 512px store illustration
its three complaints do not transfer:

- `outline_rate` fail — an inventory item on a transparent background needs a dark
  outline. A store icon on a flat field does not have one, and none of the family's
  icons has one either.
- `palette` warn (114–1218 colours) — these are the anti-aliasing blends from the
  4× reduction, not painted colours. Measured properly (tones covering ≥0.2% of
  the frame) the four use 8–25 flat colours, inside the family's own 3–23 range.
- `ramp_depth` / `jitter` warn — 16×16 texture idioms about shading depth and
  surface noise; a flat-illustration icon is not built out of either.

Negative-list check before drawing and after: no glow, no radial gradient, no
purple-blue gradient, no status dots. Every material colour is sampled from the
vanilla 1.21.1 texture named beside the constant in the render script, read out of
the asset cache — nothing drawn from memory. The two colours with no texture to
come from (night sky, dusk band) are marked as stated choices with the reasoning
attached.

## Not done here

No winner is picked; that is kura's. Nothing has been made final, no size variant
has been promoted to `branding/meanwhile_icon_*.png`, and no in-jar logo has been
wired — those belong to the finalisation step after a choice, together with the
`logoFile` entry the playbook's gate requires.
